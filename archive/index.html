haycorte

haycorte

Urban disruption intelligence for Bogotá utility outages.

Real communication problem; the gap is real. The business shape isn't.

WhatsApp-first infrastructure intelligence for utility outages (water/electricity/gas) in Bogotá. Real communication problem; the gap is real. The distribution problem killed it: getting users to install a WhatsApp bot before they need it — not after their water cuts out — requires sustained marketing that wasn't justified by the project's scope. Archived as a validated insight, not a failure of execution.

Haycorte was a WhatsApp-first service that surfaced real-time water, electricity, and gas outage data for Bogotá neighborhoods by scraping public utility schedules. Built to solve a real communication gap. Killed after validating that the distribution problem — getting users to install a WhatsApp bot before they needed it, not after their water cut out — required a marketing investment inconsistent with the project's scope. The infrastructure worked; the business shape didn't.

  • Infrastructure dependence
  • Business-structure mismatch
  • Episodic engagement
  • Capital vs. operational burden

strategically interesting as a future utility-data layer. for now: archived.

klipper-copilot

klipper-copilot

3D-printing AI copilot for Klipper firmware.

Optimizing 3D prints is a small enough niche that maintaining the copilot costs more than the printers it helps.

AI-assisted optimization layer for 3D printing workflows on Klipper-based machines. Killed after discovering that advanced Klipper users had already built strong manual tuning workflows and actively resisted handing them off. The niche was real but the audience's relationship with that niche made automation feel like a loss of control, not a gain. Archived as a lesson in audience-problem fit.

Klipper Copilot was an AI-assisted optimization layer for 3D printing workflows on Klipper-based printers, automating calibration suggestions and slicer parameter tuning. Killed after discovering that the target user population — advanced 3D printing enthusiasts — had already developed strong manual workflows and actively resisted automation of the tuning process. The problem existed; the willingness to delegate it didn't.

  • Niche audience
  • Maintenance overhead
  • Hardware fragmentation
  • Limited willingness-to-pay

archived as a learning project. potential future revival if the 3D-print ecosystem consolidates.
